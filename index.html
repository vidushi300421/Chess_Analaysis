How Fast Improvers Break Away

Using online games from Lichess, I followed two groups of players: people who rocket from ~500 to ~900, and people who get stuck. This is a three-act story about patterns, preparation, and adaptability.

Act 1

Opening Scene: The First 30 Games

We start with the very beginning: the first 30 games of 20 players – 10 fast improvers and 10 plateau players. How chaotic are their results? How many wins do they actually stack up, and how often do they beat stronger opponents?

1. A Season-Style Map of Every Result

Each row is a player, each column is a game. Blue = win, red = loss, green = draw. Fast improvers are on top, plateau players below.

2. Total Wins in the First 30 Games

Among the same 10 fast and 10 plateau players, how many wins do they collect in this opening stretch?

3. Upset Wins vs Higher-Rated Opponents

Now we only count wins against higher-rated opponents – the “punching above your weight” games.

Act 2

Middle Game: Where Do They Actually Win?

Next we move inside the games themselves. Are fast improvers just good at quick opening traps, or do they carry an edge through the middlegame and endgame too? And how much opening theory are they successfully navigating?

4. Win Rate by Game Phase

We classify games as short (opening), medium (middlegame), or long (endgame) based on total moves, then compare win rates by group.

5. Win Rate by Opening Depth

Grouping openings by how many book moves are played (very shallow to deep), we see how often each group converts those games.

Act 3

Endgame: Off-Book Chaos & Resilience

Finally we look at what happens once players leave preparation and enter chaos – and what happens emotionally after they lose.

6. Win Rate After Going Off-Book

For games where we detect an “off-book” move, how often do players still win, depending on whether they diverge early, mid, or late?

7. Off-Book Upset Rate vs Stronger Opponents

Restricting to games against higher-rated opponents, this is “creative chaos that actually works”.

8. Bounce-Back Resilience: Win After a Loss

Among games that come immediately after a loss, what fraction are wins? This is a simple measure of emotional recovery for each group.
